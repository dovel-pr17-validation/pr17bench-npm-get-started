You do not need to know every subject to help with homework. What helps most is a routine, interest, and encouragement.

![A child doing homework at a tidy table after school, with a clock and a parent nearby.](02-homework-routines-that-help.svg)

## A time and a place

- Choose a **regular time** each day, for example after a snack and a short break.
- Find a **quiet spot** with light, away from the television, even if it is the kitchen table.
- Keep pens, pencils, and paper together so time is not lost looking for them.

## Helping well

- **Ask what the task is**, and let your child explain it to you. Explaining helps them understand.
- **Guide, do not do it for them.** Ask questions like "What do you already know?" or "Where could you look?"
- **Praise effort**, not only right answers: "You kept trying until you got it."
- Break big tasks into small steps, with short breaks.

## When your child is stuck

- Look at the textbook or class notes together.
- Ask an older sibling, a neighbour, or a homework club.
- Write a short note to the teacher saying what your child found hard. See [[Talking with your child's teacher]].

## Keep an eye on

- The child's homework book or diary, to see what is due.
- Signs that homework is always too hard, or always too easy; tell the teacher.
