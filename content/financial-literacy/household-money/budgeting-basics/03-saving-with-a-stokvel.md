A stokvel is a group of people who each pay an agreed amount into a shared fund, regularly. The money is then paid out to members in turn, or at the end of the year, or used for an agreed purpose. There are hundreds of thousands of stokvels in South Africa.

![Members of a stokvel sitting in a circle, each putting money into a shared box.](03-saving-with-a-stokvel.svg)

## Kinds of stokvel

- **Rotating:** each month one member receives the whole month's contributions.
- **Savings:** money builds up in the bank and is shared out at the end of the year.
- **Grocery:** members buy groceries in bulk together, often for December.
- **Burial:** members help each other with funeral costs.
- **Investment:** the group invests together, for example in property or shares.

## Signs of a well-run stokvel

- **A written constitution** setting out the name, aims, members, contributions, payouts, officials, and rules for disputes and for members who leave. The National Stokvel Association of South Africa (NASASA) has a template.
- **Elected officials**, such as a chairperson, treasurer, and secretary.
- **A bank account in the stokvel's name**, with at least two people needed to sign for any payment. Most banks have stokvel accounts.
- **Records** of every payment, kept by the treasurer and shared with members.
- **Regular meetings**, with minutes.

> [!WARNING]
> Be careful of any "stokvel" that promises very high returns, recruits new members to pay older ones, or keeps money in one person's personal account. These are signs of a scam.
