When money runs out before month end, a quick loan can look like the only way. Some lenders take advantage of that.

![A person stepping back from a pit of debt, with a warning sign and a stack of unpaid bills.](03-avoiding-debt-traps.svg)

## Loan sharks (mashonisas)

Informal moneylenders often charge interest of 30 to 50 percent **a month**, far more than the law allows. A loan from an unregistered lender is illegal, and the National Credit Regulator says such debt cannot be enforced in court.

## It is a crime for a lender to keep your cards or ID

The National Credit Act makes it a **criminal offence** for any lender to keep your:

- **ID book or card**,
- **bank card or PIN**, or
- **SASSA card**,

as security for a loan. If a lender has your card, you can report them, and SASSA will replace a SASSA card that a lender is holding.

## Other traps to watch for

- Loans offered on WhatsApp or social media that ask for an "upfront fee" first. A real lender never asks you to pay before you get the loan.
- Taking a new loan to pay an old one. This usually makes the debt grow.
- Signing an agreement you have not read, or that has blank spaces.

## Where to get help

- Check whether a lender is registered, or report one: **National Credit Regulator, 0860 627 627**, or ncr.org.za.
- If debts are too much to manage, a **registered debt counsellor** can help arrange lower payments through debt review.
