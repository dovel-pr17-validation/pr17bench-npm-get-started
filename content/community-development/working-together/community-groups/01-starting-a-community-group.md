Many things improve faster when neighbours work together: a food garden, a street clean-up, a reading club, a savings group, or a safety patrol.

![Neighbours meeting together outdoors to plan a community project.](01-starting-a-community-group.svg)

## 1. Start with a shared need

Talk to people. What problem or goal do several of you care about? A group with one clear purpose is easier to start and keep going than one that tries to do everything.

## 2. Hold a first meeting

- Invite people personally, and put up a notice at the shop, clinic, or church.
- Choose a time and place that suits most people.
- Agree on the **purpose** of the group in one or two sentences.
- Write down who came and how to reach them.

## 3. Agree on simple rules

Write them down. A short constitution or set of rules should cover:

- the group's **name and purpose**,
- **who can be a member**, and any membership fee,
- **office bearers**, such as chairperson, secretary, and treasurer, and how they are chosen,
- **how decisions are made**, for example by majority vote at a meeting with enough members present,
- how **money** is handled; see [[Keeping group money safe]].

## 4. Start small, and celebrate

Choose one small, achievable first project. Finishing it builds trust and energy for bigger ones.

## Registering the group

Many groups never need to register. If you want to apply for grants or open certain bank accounts, you may need to register as a **non-profit organisation (NPO)** with the Department of Social Development, or as a company or trust. Ask your local Social Development office.

See also [[Running a good meeting]].
